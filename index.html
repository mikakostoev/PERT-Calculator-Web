PERT Calculator — three-point project estimates

This free PERT calculator turns optimistic, realistic and pessimistic hour ranges into a single project estimate, adjusts each task for how familiar the work is, adds a risk buffer and multiplies by your hourly rate. It runs entirely in your browser — no sign-up, no data leaves your machine.

What is a PERT estimate?

A PERT estimate is a weighted three-point estimate. You give three durations for a task — optimistic, realistic and pessimistic — and the realistic value is weighted four times as heavily as the other two:

PERT = (optimistic + 4 × realistic + pessimistic) / 6

For a task estimated at 4, 8 and 20 hours, the PERT estimate is (4 + 32 + 20) / 6 = 9.3 hours. The technique comes from the Program Evaluation and Review Technique developed for the US Navy's Polaris programme in 1958, and it is still the standard way to keep a single pessimistic outlier from dominating a plan.

What do the familiarity modes do?

Each task is multiplied by how well you know the work:

Familiarity mode Multiplier Use it when
FAMILIAR × 1.0 You have shipped this exact kind of work before
MODERATE × 1.3 You know the tools but not this particular problem
FIRST TIME × 1.7 New stack, new domain, or unknown integration

How much risk buffer should I add?

The buffer is a percentage added to the total after the familiarity multipliers, and it defaults to 30%. Raise it when requirements are still vague or a third party is involved; lower it for repeat work you have delivered several times.

How to use the calculator

  1. Set your hourly rate and the risk buffer percentage.
  2. Add a task and name it.
  3. Enter optimistic, realistic and pessimistic hours for that task.
  4. Tap the familiarity label to cycle between FAMILIAR, MODERATE and FIRST TIME.
  5. Read the total estimate and buffered hours at the top of the page.

How the total is calculated

Every task is reduced to its PERT hours, multiplied by its familiarity factor and summed. The risk buffer is then applied to that sum, and the buffered hours are multiplied by the hourly rate to produce the cost estimate shown as the current estimate.
